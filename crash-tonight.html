Financial Math · Ch I

The 6 formulas (your slides write P for C)

What you wantFormula
InterestI = C × r × n
Acquired (future) valueA = C + I = C × (1 + r × n)
Present value (capital today)C = A ÷ (1 + r × n)
Rater = I ÷ (C × n)
Timen = I ÷ (C × r)
Average rate of several placementsr = Σ(C × r × n) ÷ Σ(C × n)

n: always in YEARS

  • Days → n = days ÷ 360 (ordinary/commercial interest, the default)
  • Exact interest → n = days ÷ 365, only when the question says "exact"
  • Months → n = months ÷ 12
  • Rate: 6% → r = 0.06

Counting days: the #1 source of mistakes

  • Do not count the first day, do count the last day.
  • Example, 23 April → 9 August: April 30 − 23 = 7, May 31, June 30, July 31, August 9 → 108 days.
  • February has 28 days in 2026 and 2027, 29 in 2028.
  • Months with 31 days: Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec.
Traps: counting the first day · using 365 when it's not "exact" · 6 instead of 0.06 · months ÷ 360 · giving I when they ask A · a simple average of the rates instead of the weighted one · present value = A − I computed on A (wrong: divide by 1 + r × n).

Quick concepts

  • Time value of money: 1 TND today is worth more than 1 TND tomorrow (you can invest it; risk; inflation).
  • Simple interest is computed on the original capital only; compound interest adds interest to the capital.
  • Ordinary interest (360) gives a slightly bigger interest than exact interest (365).
Micro · Ch 1

Chapter 1 in 10 points

  • Economics: how we use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants. Scarcity = wants > resources (rich countries too).
  • Opportunity cost = the value of the next best alternative given up. Only ONE, never the sum.
  • "No free lunch": using resources for anything has an opportunity cost. Highway → the other goods sacrificed.
  • Micro = one household, firm or market. Macro = whole economy (GDP, inflation, unemployment, growth).
  • Factors and payments: land → rent, labor → wages, capital → interest, entrepreneurship → profit. Money is NOT capital; buildings and machines ARE capital.
  • 3 questions: what, how, for whom. Needs (survival) vs wants.
  • Goods: free/economic, consumer/capital, durable/non-durable, private (rival, excludable)/public (non-rival, non-excludable). Services can't be stored.
  • MB vs MC: MB > MC → more · MB < MC → fewer · MB = MC → best point.
  • Positive = fact, can be tested ("is"). Normative = opinion ("should", "ought", "must", "will harm", "too", "fair").
  • PPC: on the curve = efficient · inside = unemployment/inefficient · outside = impossible now, needs growth. Bowed out = increasing opportunity cost. More capital goods today → faster growth tomorrow.
PPC calculation: the opportunity cost of the n-th unit = how many units of the other good you lose moving ONE step. Count the difference between two neighbouring rows, never the total.
Micro · Ch 2

Chapter 2: your teacher's version

  • 3 systems: laissez-faire (private, free markets, government only protects property and contracts) · command (state owns, central planning board sets targets) · market = mixed (private markets + limited but active government).
  • 6 characteristics: private property · freedom of enterprise & choice · self-interest · competition · markets & prices · technology & capital goods (+ specialization, division of labor, money).
  • Barter needs a coincidence of wants. Money = medium of exchange accepted by all.
  • Market failure (pollution, no lighthouse) vs government failure (a useless bridge from bad planning).
  • 5 questions: what (consumer sovereignty, "dinar votes") · how (least cost) · who gets the output (income from resources) · how will it change (creative destruction, capital accumulation) · how to deal with risk (businesses bear it: profit = reward, loss = penalty).
  • Least-cost: total cost = labor × wage + capital × rental. Wage 8, rental 6: A (10 L, 2 K) = 92 · B (6 L, 5 K) = 78 · C (3 L, 9 K) = 78 → B and C tie.
  • Invisible hand (Adam Smith): self-interest + competition → good for society, without intending it. Fails with pollution, monopoly, information gaps.
  • Command failure: coordination problem (planners can't handle the information) · incentive problem (no profit, no reason to innovate).
  • Circular flow: product market (top) = households BUY goods, money to businesses · resource market (bottom) = households SELL land, labor, capital; firms pay wages, rent, interest, profit.
Least-cost = the lowest total cost, NOT the fewest workers or machines. Households SELL in the resource market and BUY in the product market.
