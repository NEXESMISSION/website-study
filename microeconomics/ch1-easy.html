Economics in 2 minutes

Welcome. You do not need to know anything before you start. We go in tiny steps.

Read a few lines. Answer a quick question. You see right away if you are right, and why.

How to use this page

Click an answer. If it is wrong, read the reason and press Try again. Do every question: they are the real lesson. When a part is clear, click I understand this part.

Tonight you have 3 free hours. You can study for the micro exam. You can work at a café and earn some money. Or you can watch the match with your friends.

You cannot do all three. You must choose.

That is economics. Not money first: choices.

DefinitionEconomics (l'économie)

The science that studies how individuals, businesses and societies make choices to use limited resources in order to satisfy their needs and wants.

Three key words in this definition:

  • Choices: economics is about deciding.
  • Limited resources: money, time, land, water, workers, machines. There is never enough.
  • Needs and wants: what people would like to have. This list never ends.
We cannot have everything, so we must choose. Economics is the science of CHOICE.
Memory trick

“Limited things + many wants = a choice.” Each time you see the word economics, think of this little sum.

Worked example · your morning

You have 10 DT and 40 minutes before class. A taxi costs 6 DT and takes 15 minutes. The bus costs 0.5 DT and takes 35 minutes.

Step 1. What is limited? Your money (10 DT) and your time (40 minutes).

Step 2. What do you want? Time to revise before class, and money for lunch.

Step 3. Bus: 9.5 DT left for lunch, but 20 minutes less to revise. Taxi: 20 more minutes to revise, but only 4 DT left for lunch.

You cannot have both. You choose. That is an economic decision.

In French: economics = l'économie · choice = le choix · resources = les ressources · needs = les besoins · wants = les désirs.

Quick check

Economics only studies money and banks.

No. Economics studies how we use any limited resource: time, water, land, workers. Money is only one of them. (This is the first question of the teacher's slide quiz.)
Q2Multiple choice

Economics is a social science concerned with…

Economics = using scarce resources in the best way to satisfy wants as much as possible. “Increasing resources” misses the idea of choice. “Minimum cost” talks about cost, not satisfaction. (End-of-chapter sheet.)
Q3True or false

Every choice has a cost, even when you pay no money.

Yes. When you choose one thing, you give up another thing: time, sleep, fun. That sacrifice is a cost, even with no money.
Q4Multiple choice

You have only 10 DT and only 40 minutes. These are the limited resources you must share between your wants.
Q5Multiple choice

An economic decision is a choice about using limited resources (here, your time). The others are nature: nobody chooses them.
Q6Multiple choice

In French, “economics” is…

Economics = l'économie (or la science économique). La rareté = scarcity, le marché = the market.
02

Scarcity and choice

Why must we choose? Because of one word: scarcity.

DefinitionScarcity (la rareté)

A situation in which human wants for goods, services and resources are greater than what is available to satisfy them.

Scarcity: Wants > Available resources

Resources are limited: land, water, workers, machines, money, time.

Wants are unlimited: when one want is satisfied, a new one comes. You buy your first phone. Then you want a better camera. Then a newer model. Then wireless earphones.

The economic problem = UNLIMITED wants vs SCARCE resources.
Memory trick

“Wants are a bag with no bottom. Resources are a small box.” The small box can never fill the bag. So we must choose what to put in.

Common mistake · scarcity ≠ poverty

Scarcity does not mean “being poor”. The richest person in the world also has only 24 hours a day. Rich countries also have limited land, water and workers. Everyone faces scarcity.

Economists also separate needs and wants:

Needs (les besoins)Wants (les désirs)
Meaningbasic things to survivethings that make life nicer, not essential
Examplesfood, water, shelter, safetya new phone, a trip to Djerba, designer sneakers
Worked example 1 · water in Tunisia (from class)

Step 1. What is the scarce resource? Water (dry summers, low dams).

Step 2. Who wants it? Farms (olives, vegetables), families, factories, hotels.

Step 3. What is the trade-off? More water for farms = less water for families and hotels.

Night water cuts in summer are a choice made because of scarcity.

Worked example 2 · a student's day

Ali has 24 hours. Sleep 8 h, classes 6 h, transport 2 h. Free time = 24 − 8 − 6 − 2 = 8 hours.

He wants: study 4 h + work at the café 4 h + football 2 h + phone 2 h = 12 hours.

Wants (12 h) > resources (8 h). That is scarcity. He must give up 12 − 8 = 4 hours of his wants.

In French: scarcity = la rareté · the economic problem = le problème économique · trade-off = un arbitrage · needs = les besoins · wants = les désirs.

False. Every country has limited land, workers and time compared with what its people want. Rich countries face scarcity too.

The economic problem = unlimited wants vs scarce resources. It exists in every society, rich or poor, with or without a government.

Needs are basic things to survive: food, water, shelter, safety. The others make life nicer but are not essential.

True. Wants are unlimited: after the first phone comes the wish for a better camera, a newer model… That is why scarcity never ends.

Wants − resources = 12 − 8 = 4 hours. He cannot do everything: he must drop 4 hours of activities.

Q15Multiple choice

03

Opportunity cost

Scarcity means every choice has a price. Not always a money price.

When you choose one thing, you give up another. What you give up has a name.

DefinitionOpportunity cost (le coût d'opportunité)

The value of the next best alternative that you give up when you make a choice.

Two small words matter a lot on the exam:

  • “Next best”: only the one best option you gave up. You never add all the options.
  • “Value”: not always money. It can be time, sleep, fun.
Memory trick

“Opportunity cost = the BEST thing you did NOT choose.” One thing only. Never a sum.

Worked example 1 · Youssef's evening

Tonight Youssef can (1) work 4 hours at a café for 5 DT per hour, (2) help his uncle at his shop for 15 DT, or (3) study micro. He studies.

Step 1. What did he choose? Studying.

Step 2. What did he give up? Café = 4 × 5 = 20 DT. Uncle's shop = 15 DT.

Step 3. Keep only the BEST one: the café, 20 DT.

Answer: opportunity cost = 20 DT. Not 20 + 15 = 35 DT: he could do only one of them.

Worked example 2 · Sami (slide exercise)

Sami can work a weekend shift for 60 DT or tutor a neighbour's child for 40 DT. He tutors.

Opportunity cost = 60 DT (the shift he gave up). The 40 DT is what he gets, not what he gives up.

Why choose tutoring then? Non-money value: he likes teaching, he helps the neighbour. Rational people count all benefits, not only money.

Three examples from the lecture:

  • A student studies all evening → gives up a party with friends, or sleep.
  • A city builds a hospital → gives up the new school it could build with the same budget.
  • A business starts a new product line → gives up extra marketing for a product it already sells.
Common mistake · adding the options

Yasmine can work at a shop (50 DT), babysit (35 DT) or go to the beach. She goes to the beach. Opportunity cost = 50 DT, not 50 + 35 = 85 DT.

A famous sentence: “There is no such thing as a free lunch.”

It means: even “free” things use resources. A free public road uses workers, cement and land that could make something else.

So the opportunity cost of a new highway is the other goods and services we could have made instead (a hospital, a school). It is not just the money paid to the builders.

In French: opportunity cost = le coût d'opportunité · alternative = une option · to give up = renoncer à.

Quick check

Q16Multiple choice

Sami can work a shift for 60 DT or tutor for 40 DT. He tutors. His opportunity cost is…

Opportunity cost = the value of what he gave up: the 60 DT shift. 40 DT is what he gets.
Q17Multiple choice

Yasmine can earn 50 DT at a shop or 35 DT babysitting. She goes to the beach. The opportunity cost of the beach is…

Only the best alternative counts: 50 DT. Adding both (85) is the most common mistake. The beach costs no money, but it has an opportunity cost.
Q18Multiple choice

“There is no such thing as a free lunch” means that…

Even a “free” meal uses food, workers and energy that could have made something else. That sacrifice is an opportunity cost. (End-of-chapter sheet.)
Q19Multiple choice

The opportunity cost of a new public highway is…

Opportunity cost always asks: “what else could we have had?” The money paid to builders is only the money cost: a classic trap. (End-of-chapter sheet.)
Q20True or false

Opportunity cost is always an amount of money.

False. It is the value of the best alternative: it can be sleep, time, fun or a party you missed.
Q21Multiple choice

Rim studies for 2 hours. Without studying, she would sleep (her favourite option) or watch a series. Her opportunity cost is…

Sleep is the next best alternative, so it is the opportunity cost. Never add the options.
Q22Multiple choice

Nour spends 2 hours at the beach. She could earn 7 DT per hour at a café or 5 DT per hour at a bakery. Her opportunity cost is…

Best alternative = café: 2 × 7 = 14 DT. Not 14 + 10 = 24 (adding options). Not 7 (that is only one hour).
Q23True or false

If you choose the best option, you have no opportunity cost.

False. Every choice gives up something. Even the best choice has a cost: the next best option you did not take.
Q24Multiple choice

Which one is part of the opportunity cost of studying full-time at university?

The salary you give up is the classic opportunity cost of studying. Food you eat anyway is not a sacrifice; the diploma and friends are benefits, not costs.
Q25Multiple choice

A farmer grows olives on a field. The same field could grow wheat worth 3,000 DT or tomatoes worth 2,000 DT. The opportunity cost of the olives is…

The next best use of the field is wheat (3,000 DT). Owning the land does not make it free: it could grow something else.
Q26Multiple choice

The city of Sousse uses its budget to build a new hospital. The same money could build a new school (its next best project). The opportunity cost of the hospital is…

Opportunity cost = the next best alternative given up with the same budget: the school. (Lecture example.)
Q27True or false

The opportunity cost of the same choice can change when your alternatives change.

True. If Yasmine's shop job disappears, the best alternative becomes babysitting, so the cost of the beach falls from 50 DT to 35 DT.
Q28Multiple choice

In French, “opportunity cost” is…

Opportunity cost = le coût d'opportunité. Le coût marginal = marginal cost (later in this lesson).

Try on paper first

P1Problem

Salma has a free Saturday (8 hours). She can work at a café for 5 DT per hour, give 2 private lessons at 25 DT each, or go to the beach (she values the beach at 30 DT). (a) She goes to the beach. What is her opportunity cost? (b) The lessons are cancelled, and she still goes to the beach. Opportunity cost now? (c) Another Saturday she gives the lessons. Opportunity cost?

Show the full solution

First, value each option. Café: 8 × 5 = 40 DT. Lessons: 2 × 25 = 50 DT. Beach: 30 DT.

(a) Beach chosen. Options given up: café 40 DT, lessons 50 DT. Best one = lessons → opportunity cost = 50 DT.

(b) Lessons gone. Only the café is left → opportunity cost = 40 DT. Same choice, new alternatives, new cost.

(c) Lessons chosen. Given up: café 40 DT, beach 30 DT. Best one = café → opportunity cost = 40 DT. Never 40 + 30.

P2Problem

The State has 200 million DT. It can build a football stadium, a desalination plant (its second favourite project) or new schools (its third). It builds the stadium. (a) What is the opportunity cost? (b) A student says: “The opportunity cost is the plant plus the schools.” Correct him. (c) Another student says: “The opportunity cost is the 200 million DT.” Is he right?

Show the full solution

(a) The next best alternative: the desalination plant (the water it would have given Tunisia).

(b) Wrong: the State could build only one project with this money. Opportunity cost = the single best alternative, never the sum.

(c) Not really. 200 million DT is the money (accounting) cost. The opportunity cost is what society gives up: the plant that could have been built with those resources. “There is no such thing as a free lunch.”

04

Micro vs macro

Economics has two branches. Same economy, looked at from two distances.

DefinitionMicroeconomics (la microéconomie)

The study of individual economic units: households, firms and markets.

  • Households (consumers) decide what to buy and how much to save. They want the most satisfaction (utility).
  • Firms decide what to produce and at what price. They want the most profit.
  • Markets are where buyers and sellers meet and set prices.
DefinitionMacroeconomics (la macroéconomie)

The study of the economy as a whole: national totals, not individual decisions.

Macro words: GDP (total production, le PIB), inflation (the general price level), the unemployment rate, economic growth, interest and exchange rates of the country.

Memory trick

Micro = microscope: you zoom on ONE household, ONE firm, ONE market. Macro = satellite: you see the WHOLE country. Macro words: national, total, the economy, inflation, unemployment rate, GDP.

Trap · a big company is still micro

Tunisie Télécom, Ooredoo, Intel: one firm → micro, even if it is huge. One bank's interest rate on its loans → micro. Macro is only for the totals of the whole economy.

Worked example · sort in 2 steps

“The price of olive oil in Sfax rises after a bad harvest.”

Step 1. What is it about? One product, one market. Step 2. One unit → micro.

“Tunisia's inflation rate is 6%.”

Step 1. What is it about? The general price level of the whole country. Step 2. Whole economy → macro.

In French: la microéconomie · la macroéconomie · household = le ménage · firm = l'entreprise · GDP = le PIB · unemployment = le chômage.

05

The 4 factors of production

To make anything (bread, a phone, a haircut) you need resources.

Economists put all resources into 4 groups. They are called the factors of production.

DefinitionFactors of production (les facteurs de production)

The economic resources used to produce goods and services: land, labor, capital and entrepreneurship. They are all scarce.

FactorWhat it isExamplesPayment
Land (la terre)natural resources, “gifts of nature”farmland, forests, oil, phosphate, water, fish in the seaRent
Labor (le travail)human effort, physical and mentala barista, a teacher, an engineerWages
Capital (le capital)man-made tools used to produce other goodsmachines, tools, factories, delivery vans, computersInterest
Entrepreneurship (l'entrepreneuriat)organises the other three, takes risks, has new ideasthe person who starts and runs the businessProfit
Memory trick

The 4 factors in order: Land, Labor, Capital, Entrepreneurship. Payments in the same order: R-W-I-P.

“Rent the land, Work for wages, Interest on machines, Profit for the boss.”

Trap 1 · money is NOT capital

In economics, capital = physical tools and machines. The money a firm borrows from a bank is “financial capital”, not a factor of production. Money alone makes nothing. What you buy with it (an oven, a truck) is capital.

Trap 2 · buildings

A factory, a shop or an office building is man-made → capital. Only the natural ground (and natural resources like oil, water, fish) is land.

One more word. In economics, investment (l'investissement) = spending on new capital goods (machines, buildings). Buying shares at the stock exchange is a financial investment, not investment in the economic sense.

Worked example 1 · Amira's café in Sfax

Step 1. What comes from nature? The site of the shop downtown → land → paid rent.

Step 2. Who works? Two baristas → labor → paid wages.

Step 3. Which man-made tools? The espresso machine, the chairs → capital → interest.

Step 4. Who has the idea and takes the risk? Amira → entrepreneurship → profit (if the café succeeds).

Worked example 2 · farmer Hédi's olive grove

Land = the olive grove and the rain. Labor = the pickers in November. Capital = the tractor and the olive press. Entrepreneurship = Hédi, who decides what to plant and risks his savings.

In French: les facteurs de production · la terre · le travail · le capital · l'entrepreneuriat · rent = la rente · wages = les salaires · interest = l'intérêt · profit = le profit.

06

Goods and services: consumer vs capital

The economy produces goods (things you can touch) and services (activities: a haircut, a lesson, a louage ride).

The most important pair for the exam: consumer goods vs capital goods.

DefinitionConsumer goods (les biens de consommation)

Goods that satisfy wants directly: food, clothes, a phone for yourself.

DefinitionCapital goods (les biens de capital, biens d'équipement)

Goods used to produce other goods and services: an oven, a tractor, a taxi car, a sewing machine.

Memory trick

Ask one question: “Is it used to MAKE something else?” Yes → capital good. No, people use it for themselves → consumer good.

Trap · same object, different use

A car for a family = consumer good. The same car used as a taxi = capital good. A laptop to watch films = consumer good; a designer's laptop to earn money = capital good. Always ask: who uses it, and for what?

Worked example · classify 3 things

The bakery's oven: does it make something else? Yes, bread → capital good.

The bread you eat: it makes nothing else, you eat it → consumer good.

The louage van: it carries passengers for money (a service) → capital good.

Other pairs from class (short):

PairFirst typeSecond type
Free vs economicfree: unlimited, no cost (air, sunlight)economic: scarce (food, cars)
Durable vs non-durabledurable: lasts long (fridge, car)non-durable: used quickly (bread, fuel)
Private vs publicprivate: rival and excludable (a pizza)public: non-rival and non-excludable (streetlights, national defence)

Services (les services) cannot be touched or stored. A barber cannot make 50 haircuts today and keep them for next week.

Finally, because resources are scarce, every society must answer 3 questions: What to produce? (more consumer goods or more capital goods?) How to produce? (with many workers or with machines?) For whom? (who gets the output?)

In French: biens de consommation · biens de capital (d'équipement) · biens durables · biens publics · les services.

Q62Multiple choice

Workers or machines is a choice of method: “How to produce?”.
Q63Multiple choice

“For whom” is about who gets the output. Here: according to need.
07

Marginal analysis: MB vs MC

Economists assume people are rational: they try to get the most well-being. Consumers want the most satisfaction (utility). Firms want the most profit.

How do they decide how much to do? Not “all or nothing”. Step by step: “one more?”

In economics, marginal means one more, the extra unit.

DefinitionMarginal benefit (MB, le bénéfice marginal)

The extra gain from one more unit of something.

DefinitionMarginal cost (MC, le coût marginal)

The extra cost of that one more unit.

MB > MC → do it · MB < MC → stop · Best point: MB = MC
Memory trick

“Is the NEXT one worth it?” Look only at the next unit, not the whole. Like msemen at breakfast: the 1st is great, the 4th is too much.

The teacher's example · one more cookie?

A bakery asks: make one more cookie?

The cookie earns 2 dinars (MB) and costs 1 dinar (MC) to make. MB > MC → yes, make it (+1 dinar).

The next cookie would cost 2 dinars (MC) and earn only 1 dinar (MB). MC > MB → no, it would lose 1 dinar.

Worked example 1 · the food truck (slide exercise)
SandwichMB (DT)MC (DT)MB − MC
1st83+5
2nd64+2
3rd550
4th36−3

Step 1. Compute MB − MC for each sandwich (last column).

Step 2. Make it while MB > MC: 1st and 2nd.

Step 3. Stop where MB = MC: the 3rd. Best quantity = 3 sandwiches.

Step 4. The 4th loses 3 DT (MC > MB): do not make it. Total net benefit: 5 + 2 + 0 = 7 DT. With a 4th: 7 − 3 = 4 DT.

Worked example 2 · your study tonight

You studied 3 hours. Do a 4th? MB = the extra marks on Tuesday. MC = less sleep, tired tomorrow.

Exam in 2 days, chapter not finished: MB is big → study. At 2 a.m., eyes closing: MB is small, MC is big → sleep. Not “is studying good?” but “is one more hour worth it?”.

In French: analyse marginale · bénéfice marginal · coût marginal · rationnel · utility = l'utilité.

Quick check

Q64Multiple choice

Henry wants to buy a book. The economic perspective says he will buy it if…

Rational rule: do it when MB > MC. MC > MB is when he should not buy. (End-of-chapter sheet.)
Q65More, fewer or the same?

A firm makes 3,000 units. For the next unit, MB = 13 DT and MC = 10 DT.

MB (13) > MC (10): one more unit still adds 3 DT. Produce more.
Q66More, fewer or the same?

A firm makes 11 units. MB = 3 DT and MC = 4 DT.

MC (4) > MB (3): the last unit costs more than it brings. Produce fewer.
Q67More, fewer or the same?

A firm makes 500 units. MB = 9 DT and MC = 9 DT.

MB = MC: this is the best point. Changing brings no gain. Keep the same amount.
Q68More, fewer or the same?

Q69More, fewer or the same?

A firm makes 5 units, where MB < MC.

MB < MC: the last unit is not worth it. Produce fewer.
Q70Multiple choice

Marginal = the extra (additional) unit. MB = extra benefit, MC = extra cost.

Positive vs normative

Economists say two kinds of sentences. The exam always asks you to tell them apart.

DefinitionPositive economics (l'économie positive)

Statements about what is. Facts. You can check them with data.

DefinitionNormative economics (l'économie normative)

Statements about what should be. Opinions and value judgements. Reasonable people can disagree.

PositiveNormative
Question“What is?”“What should be?”
Based ondata, factsvalues, opinions
Think ofa news reportera politician
Teacher's examples“The unemployment rate is 8%.” · “Raising the price of coffee led to fewer people buying it.” · “Company profits increased by 10%.”“The government should raise the minimum wage.” · “Taxes on the wealthy ought to be higher.”
Memory trick

Ask: “Can I check it with data?” Yes → Positive (Proof possible). Opinion words → Normative (Not a fact): should, ought to, must, too (high/hot/much), fair, better, good, bad, harm, needs to be.

Trap 1 · a false statement can be positive

“Tunisia's unemployment rate is 50%.” It is false. But you can check it with data and prove it wrong, so it is positive.

Trap 2 · hidden opinion words

“A trade deficit of 20 billion dollars will harm the economy.” It has a number, but “harm” is a judgement → normative. “It is too hot to jog” → normative. “It is 33°C” → positive.

Worked example · 2 steps

“Inflation rose to 5% last quarter.” Step 1. Any opinion word? No. Step 2. Can I check it with data? Yes → positive.

“Universities ought to lower their fees.” Step 1. “Ought to” → normative. Done.

Three more words from the textbookCeteris paribus and two thinking mistakes

Ceteris paribus (toutes choses égales par ailleurs) = “other things equal”: we change one thing and keep everything else the same.

Fallacy of composition: thinking that what is true for one is true for all. One fan stands up at the stadium and sees better; if all stand up, nobody sees better.

Post hoc, ergo propter hoc: “A came before B, so A caused B.” Not always true.

In French: l'économie positive · l'économie normative · value judgement = le jugement de valeur · toutes choses égales par ailleurs.

The budget line

Now the first graph of the chapter. It is about you and your money.

Your income is limited (scarcity). The budget line shows what you can buy with it.

DefinitionBudget line (la droite de budget)

All the combinations of two goods a person can buy with a given income and given prices, spending all the money.

Youssef is a student. Each week he has 60 DT for two things: louage tickets to visit his family (10 DT each) and sandwiches (5 DT each).

CombinationTickets (10 DT)Sandwiches (5 DT)Total spent
A6060 + 0 = 60 DT
B5250 + 10 = 60 DT
C4440 + 20 = 60 DT
D3630 + 30 = 60 DT
E2820 + 40 = 60 DT
F11010 + 50 = 60 DT
G0120 + 60 = 60 DT
Worked example 1 · the two ends of the line

Step 1. All the money on tickets: 60 ÷ 10 = 6 tickets (point A).

Step 2. All the money on sandwiches: 60 ÷ 5 = 12 sandwiches (point G).

Step 3. Join the two points with a straight line: that is the budget line.

Maximum of a good = Income ÷ its price

Three kinds of points:

  • On the line: he spends all his money. Attainable.
  • Inside the line: attainable, with money left over.
  • Outside the line: unattainable: he cannot pay.
Worked example 2 · can he afford it?

(2 tickets, 4 sandwiches): 2 × 10 + 4 × 5 = 20 + 20 = 40 DT ≤ 60 → inside, 20 DT left.

(5 tickets, 6 sandwiches): 5 × 10 + 6 × 5 = 50 + 30 = 80 DT > 60 → outside, unattainable.

Now the opportunity cost. From A to B, Youssef gives up 1 ticket and gets 2 sandwiches.

So 1 ticket = 2 sandwiches, and 1 sandwich = ½ ticket. It is the same all along the line.

Opportunity cost of 1 ticket = price of ticket ÷ price of sandwich = 10 ÷ 5 = 2 sandwiches

This is also the slope of the line. Sandwiches are on the horizontal axis: slope = rise ÷ run = −6 ÷ 12 = −½.

Same answer with prices: slope = −(price of the good on the horizontal axis ÷ price of the good on the vertical axis) = −(5 ÷ 10) = −½.

Memory trick

“Max = Money ÷ Price” and “Slope = price ratio”. The line is straight because prices do not change: the opportunity cost stays the same.

What moves the line?

  • Income rises → the whole line moves out, parallel (same slope). Income falls → it moves in.
  • The price of one good changes → the line turns (pivots) on that good's axis. The other end does not move.

In French: la droite de budget (la contrainte budgétaire) · income = le revenu · relative price = le prix relatif · slope = la pente.

The production possibilities curve (PPC)

The budget line was about one person. The PPC is the same idea for a whole country (or a farm).

A country's resources are the 4 factors of production. They are limited. So the country cannot produce everything.

DefinitionProduction possibilities curve (la frontière des possibilités de production)

A curve that shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce when it uses all its resources fully and with given technology.

The PPC has 4 assumptions:

  • Full employment: all resources are used.
  • Fixed resources: their quantity and quality do not change.
  • Fixed technology: no new methods.
  • Two goods: often consumer goods and capital goods.

Here is the table from your end-of-chapter sheet:

AlternativeABCDEF
Capital goods543210
Consumer goods059121415
PointMeaning
On the curve (A–F)efficient: all resources used. More of one good = less of the other.
Inside (U)inefficient: unemployment or wasted resources. You could have more of both.
Outside (W)unattainable: impossible today. It needs economic growth.
Memory trick

ON = Optimal (efficient). INSIDE = Idle (lazy, unused resources). OUTSIDE = Out of reach (needs growth).

Worked example 1 · opportunity cost from the table (sheet Q13)

The economy is at C (3 capital, 9 consumer). What does the 4th capital good cost?

Step 1. The 4th capital good means moving to B (4 capital, 5 consumer).

Step 2. Consumer goods fall from 9 to 5.

Step 3. Opportunity cost = 9 − 5 = 4 consumer goods.

Now go from F to A, one capital good at a time, and count what you lose:

MoveCapital good gainedConsumer goods given up
F → E1st15 − 14 = 1
E → D2nd14 − 12 = 2
D → C3rd12 − 9 = 3
C → B4th9 − 5 = 4
B → A5th5 − 0 = 5

Each extra capital good costs more than the one before: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

DefinitionLaw of increasing opportunity costs

The more you produce of one good, the more of the other good you must give up for each extra unit.

Why? Resources are not perfectly adaptable. To make more machines, Tunisia first moves the workers who are good at building machines. At the end it moves excellent bakers who are bad at machines. So it loses a lot of bread for very few machines.

The shape: increasing opportunity cost → the PPC is bowed out (concave to the origin). If the cost were constant, the PPC would be a straight line (like the budget line).

Memory trick 2

“Cost goes UP → the curve bows OUT.”

Worked example 2 · farmer Hédi: olives or wheat?
Olives (tonnes)012345

Step 2. The cost goes up: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 tonnes of wheat. Increasing opportunity cost.

Step 3. So Hédi's PPC is bowed out. Why? His best olive land is used first; at the end he plants olives on his best wheat fields.

Unemployment, growth and the PPC

The PPC is a photo of today. Two different stories can change the picture. Do not mix them.

Story 1: unemployment → a point inside

When workers have no job or factories are closed, the economy produces less than it could. It is at a point inside the curve.

The curve does not move. The resources still exist; they are just not used.

When the unemployed find jobs, the point moves toward the curve. Still no shift.

Trap · inside is not growth

A point inside = unemployment (or waste), not growth. Moving from inside to the curve is not economic growth: the PPC itself does not shift.

Story 2: economic growth → the curve shifts out

DefinitionEconomic growth (la croissance économique)

An outward shift of the PPC: the economy can now produce more of both goods.

Two causes: more resources (more workers, new land, more machines) or better technology.

The curve can also shift inward: a drought destroys farmland, a war destroys factories, many workers emigrate.

If better technology helps only one good (for example a new olive-harvesting machine), the curve stretches out only on that good's axis.

Story 3: today's choice decides tomorrow

An economy that makes more capital goods today (point C rather than D) has more machines and factories tomorrow. So its PPC shifts out faster.

The price: fewer consumer goods today (9 instead of 12).

Memory trick

“Inside = Idle. Shift out = Grow. Machines today = more tomorrow.”

Worked example · 3 events

Event 1. 20,000 unemployed graduates find jobs. Are there new resources? No, old ones are now used. → The point moves from inside toward the curve. No shift.

Event 2. New irrigation technology on farms. Better technology → the PPC shifts out.

Event 3. A drought destroys olive groves. Fewer resources → the PPC shifts in.

2. “Choosing the alternative with MORE capital goods” → faster growth.

3. A point with fewer consumer goods than the table allows → inside: inefficient (unemployment).

4. A point with more than the table allows → outside: needs economic growth.

One more idea: with international trade, a country can consume a combination outside its PPC. Trade does not shift the PPC (what it can produce stays the same).

Try on paper first

P12Problem

For each event, say what happens on the PPC graph: a point moves inside, a point moves toward the curve, a move along the curve, a shift out, or a shift in. (a) A crisis closes many factories. (b) A big oil field is discovered in the south. (c) The government chooses more machines and fewer consumer goods this year. (d) Floods destroy farmland in the north.

Show the full solution

(a) Factories still exist but stop working → unemployed resources → the point moves inside the curve. No shift.

(b) New natural resource (land) → the PPC shifts out.

(c) Today: a move along the curve (toward more capital goods). Tomorrow: the curve shifts out faster.

(d) Land destroyed → fewer resources → the PPC shifts in.

P13Problem

Countries Alpha and Beta have the same PPC (the A–F table). Alpha produces at C; Beta produces at D. (a) Which one will probably grow faster? Why? (b) What does it give up today? (c) A student says: “Beta is at a point inside, so it has unemployment.” Is he right?

Show the full solution

(a) Alpha: it makes 3 capital goods instead of 2. More machines and factories today → its PPC shifts out faster.

(b) Consumer goods today: 12 − 9 = 3 consumer goods less than Beta.

(c) No. D is on the curve: Beta is efficient, with full employment. It simply chooses more consumer goods now and less growth later.

A firm produces where MB = 15 DT and MC = 9 DT. To get the most net benefit, it should…

The payment for labor is…

Money is the factor of production called capital.

The opportunity cost of a decision is…

Next best alternative only. Not the sum, not only money.

On a PPC, a point inside the curve shows…

Income 40 DT. Sandwiches (horizontal axis) cost 4 DT; louage tickets (vertical axis) cost 8 DT. The maximum number of sandwiches is…

Same budget (40 DT, sandwich 4 DT across, ticket 8 DT up). The slope of the budget line is…

−(price across ÷ price up) = −(4 ÷ 8) = −0.5. Check with the ends: −5 ÷ 10 = −0.5.

Use the A–F table. Moving from B to A, the 5th capital good costs…

“Cost goes UP → the curve bows OUT.” Constant cost would give a straight line.

What to remember

  • Economics (l'économie) = how we use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants. The science of choice.
  • Scarcity (la rareté): wants > resources. It touches everyone, rich or poor.
  • Opportunity cost (le coût d'opportunité) = the value of the ONE best alternative given up. Never a sum.
  • Micro = one household, firm or market (even a big firm). Macro = the whole economy: GDP, inflation, unemployment, growth.
  • Factors of production: land → rent, labor → wages, capital → interest, entrepreneurship → profit (R-W-I-P). Money is not capital.
  • Capital good = used to make other goods. Consumer good = used directly. Same object, different use.
  • Marginal analysis: MB > MC → do more. MB < MC → do less. Best point: MB = MC.
  • Positive = “what is”, checkable (even if false). Normative = “should”, opinion.
  • Budget line: max = income ÷ price; slope = price ratio. Income change → parallel shift. Price change → the line turns.
  • PPC: on = efficient, inside = unemployment, outside = needs growth. Increasing opportunity cost → bowed out.
  • Growth = PPC shifts out (more resources, better technology). More capital goods today → faster growth tomorrow.
