01

Interest in 2 minutes

You lend money to someone. Later, you get your money back plus a little extra.

That extra money is the interest. It is the price of using someone else's money.

Two people: the lender gives the money. The borrower receives it and pays the interest.

You put 1,000 TND in a deposit at your bank: you are the lender, the bank pays you interest. You take a loan for a scooter: you are the borrower, you pay the interest.

Interest depends on 3 things: how much (the capital), the rate (a % per year), and how long (the time).

DefinitionSimple interest (intérêt simple)

Interest calculated only on the original capital. The interest is never added to the capital to earn more interest. So each year brings the same interest.

It is used for short-term operations: loans and deposits of less than one year.

Memory trick · Simple = Same

With simple interest, every year gives the same amount of interest. Like a staircase with equal steps.

In French: interest = intérêt · simple interest = intérêt simple · capital = capital · rate = taux · time = durée · lender = prêteur · borrower = emprunteur.

Example 1 · Amine's deposit at BIAT

Amine deposits 1,000 TND for 3 years. The bank pays 10% per year, simple interest.

Step 1. Interest for one year = 1,000 × 10% = 1,000 × 0.10 = 100 TND.

Step 2. Interest for 3 years = 3 × 100 = 300 TND.

Step 3. Amine gets back 1,000 + 300 = 1,300 TND.

Example 2 · Simple vs compound (next chapter)

Same 1,000 TND at 10%, but now the interest is added to the capital each year (compound interest, intérêt composé).

Year 1: 1,000 × 1.10 = 1,100. Year 2: 1,100 × 1.10 = 1,210. Year 3: 1,210 × 1.10 = 1,331 TND.

Simple gives 1,300. Compound gives 1,331. The extra 31 TND is interest on interest. This chapter is only simple interest.

Common mistake · The rate is per year

"8%" means 8% per year, unless the question says otherwise. For 6 months you get only half of it.

02

Time value of money

Your uncle says: "I give you 1,000 TND. Do you want it today or in one year?"

Take it today. Why? Because today you can put it in the bank, and in one year you will have more than 1,000 TND.

This is the time value of money (TVM).

DefinitionTime value of money (valeur temps de l'argent)

A sum of money available today is worth more than the same sum in the future.

1 dinar today > 1 dinar tomorrow.

Why does interest exist? 3 reasons

Interest pays the lender for three losses:

ReasonSimple meaningIn French
RiskThe borrower may not pay back.risque (de non-remboursement)
LiquidityThe lender cannot use his money during the loan. He gives up other chances.liquidité, coût d'opportunité
InflationPrices go up. Later, the same money buys less.inflation, perte de pouvoir d'achat
Memory trick · R-L-I: the 3 losses of the lender

Risk (maybe no repayment) · Liquidity (money blocked) · Inflation (money buys less).

The borrower's comfort is not a reason. Interest is always for the lender.

Common mistake · The slide trap

"Compensation for the borrower's convenience" looks nice but is wrong. It is the answer to the slide MCQ "which is NOT a reason".

To compare money at two different dates, bring it to the same date. The value today of a future sum is its present value (PV):

PV = FV / (1 + r × n)

FV = the future sum, r = the rate, n = the time in years. You will practise it in section 07.

Example 1 · Yasmine's car

Yasmine wants a car that costs 23,200 TND in 2 years. Her bank pays 8% simple interest. How much must she deposit today?

Step 1. 1 + r × n = 1 + 0.08 × 2 = 1 + 0.16 = 1.16.

Step 2. PV = 23,200 / 1.16 = 20,000 TND.

Check: 20,000 × 8% × 2 = 3,200 of interest. 20,000 + 3,200 = 23,200 ✓.

Example 2 · Pay the supplier now or in 60 days?

A shop in Sfax receives a bill of 20,000 TND. Two choices: pay the full 20,000 in 60 days, or pay today with a 2% discount: 20,000 − 400 = 19,600 TND. Money earns 6% per year.

Step 1. Bring the 20,000 back to today: 1 + 0.06 × 60/360 = 1 + 0.01 = 1.01.

Step 2. PV = 20,000 / 1.01 = 19,801.980 TND.

Step 3. Compare today's values: paying today costs 19,600. Paying later costs 19,801.980 in today's money. Paying today is cheaper.

In French: time value of money = valeur temps de l'argent · present value = valeur actuelle · future value = valeur future / valeur acquise.

Memory trick · Need or have?

Need money → short-term loan. Have money → T-bill, CD or savings.

Safest = T-bill (the State pays). Most liquid = savings account. Blocked with a penalty = CD. Bought below face value = T-bill.

Example 1 · A shop waiting for its client

A clothes shop in Sfax must pay its suppliers today. Its big client will pay in 2 months.

The shop takes a cash-flow loan (crédit de trésorerie) at BIAT, Attijari Bank or STB, and repays it when the client pays. This is a short-term loan.

Example 2 · A T-bill

A T-bill has a face value of 10,000 TND (paid at maturity, in 180 days). You buy it today for 9,850 TND.

Interest = face value − price = 10,000 − 9,850 = 150 TND. Nobody pays you "interest" separately: you earn it by buying below face value.

04

Counting days (the #1 source of mistakes)

The rate is per year. But many loans last some days, from one date to another date.

So you must count the exact number of days. Most wrong answers on the test come from one wrong day.

Do NOT count the first day. DO count the last day.

Think of a hotel. You arrive on 10 March and leave on 13 March. You pay 3 nights, not 4.

Money works the same way: the deposit day does not earn interest; the last day does. So 10 March → 13 March = 13 − 10 = 3 days.

The method in 3 steps

  1. First month: days left = (days in that month) − (start date).
  2. Months in between: add each full month (31, 30 or 28/29).
  3. Last month: add the end date's day number.

Same month? Just do end date − start date.

Memory trick · The knuckles

Make a fist. Say the months on your knuckles and the valleys between them. Knuckle = 31 days, valley = 30 (or February).

Jan (knuckle) · Feb (valley) · Mar (knuckle) · Apr (valley) · May (knuckle) · Jun (valley) · Jul (last knuckle), then start again on the first knuckle: Aug (knuckle) · Sep · Oct · Nov · Dec. July and August are both 31.

When the time is given in months

If the question gives months ("for 6 months"), use n = months / 12. Do not count days.

The end date has the same day number: 6 months after 1 January = 1 July. 3 months after 15 March = 15 June.

If the question gives two dates, count the real days and use n = days / 360.

Common mistake · 4 classic day mistakes

1. Counting the first day: you get one day too many (168 instead of 167).

2. Giving 30 days to every month. Count the real month lengths.

3. Forgetting that February 2026 and 2027 have 28 days, and February 2028 has 29.

4. When the question asks "on what date", stopping at the number of days. The answer must be a date.

I = C × r × n

Here is the one formula of the chapter. Everything else comes from it.

I = C × r × n
Exam alert · C = P

On this page the capital is called C. Your teacher's slides, the full lesson and the Moodle test call it P (principal). C = P: same letter meaning, same formulas. So I = C × r × n is the slides' I = P × r × n, A = C × (1 + r × n) is A = P × (1 + r × n), and C = A / (1 + r × n) is P = A / (1 + r × n). When the test writes P (or PV for the present value), read C.

Memory trick · Percent = divide by 100

7% → 0.07 · 9% → 0.09 · 12.5% → 0.125 · 8.4% → 0.084. Move the decimal point 2 places to the left.

Months go with 12, days go with 360. Never mix them.

Example 1 · Karim's scooter loan

Karim borrows 3,000 TND for a scooter, for 8 months, at 9%.

Step 1. r = 9% = 0.09. n = 8/12 (months → divide by 12).

Step 2. C × r = 3,000 × 0.09 = 270 (the interest for one full year).

Step 3. I = 270 × 8/12 = 2,160 / 12 = 180 TND.

Example 2 · The teacher's example: 10,000 TND at 7%, 23 April → 9 August

Step 1. Days = 7 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 9 = 108 (section 04).

Step 2. n = 108/360 = 0.3.

Step 3. I = 10,000 × 0.07 × 0.3 = 700 × 0.3 = 210 TND.

Example 3 · Exercise 1, part 1: 28,000 TND at 9%, 13/09 → 27/02

Step 1. Days = 17 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 27 = 167.

Step 2. One year of interest: 28,000 × 0.09 = 2,520.

Step 3. I = 2,520 × 167/360 = 420,840 / 360 = 1,169 TND.

Common mistake · 3 traps that the MCQ options use

1. Using 9 instead of 0.09: the answer is 100 times too big.

2. Dividing months by 360 or days by 12.

3. Counting the first day (168 days → 1,176 instead of 1,169) or using 365 (→ 1,152.986).

Exam alert · Wrong answers are planned

In an MCQ, the wrong options are the results of these exact mistakes. If your answer is in the list, it is not proof that it is right. Check the days, the decimal and the 360 again.

In French: simple interest formula = formule de l'intérêt simple · commercial year = année commerciale (360 jours) · annual rate = taux annuel.

Future value and present value

Future value: you know the money today. How much will it be later?

Present value: you know the money later. How much is it worth today?

DefinitionAcquired value, future value (valeur acquise), A

The capital plus its interest at the end: what you have (or must repay) at maturity.

A = C + I = C × (1 + r × n)

Slides (F4): A = P × (1 + r × n) = P + I. Same formula, because C = P.

DefinitionPresent value (valeur actuelle), C, P or PV

The value today of a sum A received or paid later.

C = A / (1 + r × n)

Slides (F5): P = A / (1 + r × n). Same formula, because C = P.

Memory trick · Forward = multiply, back = divide

Going forward in time (today → later): multiply by (1 + r × n).

Going back in time (later → today): divide by (1 + r × n).

Example 1 · Future value: the teacher's example

10,000 TND at 7% for 108 days. What is the acquired value?

Step 1. r × n = 0.07 × 108/360 = 0.07 × 0.3 = 0.021.

Step 2. 1 + r × n = 1.021.

Step 3. A = 10,000 × 1.021 = 10,210 TND. Same as C + I = 10,000 + 210 ✓.

Example 2 · Present value: saving for the summer

Rania needs 5,000 TND in 9 months for a summer trip. Her bank pays 6%. How much must she place today?

Step 1. n = 9/12 = 0.75. r × n = 0.06 × 0.75 = 0.045.

Step 2. 1 + r × n = 1.045.

Step 3. C = 5,000 / 1.045 = 4,784.689 TND.

Check: 4,784.689 × 1.045 = 5,000 ✓.

Example 3 · Exercise 1, part 4: find the capital

A capital is placed at 8.4% for 62 days. Its acquired value is 16,738.700 TND. Find the capital.

Step 1. r × n = 0.084 × 62/360 = 5.208 / 360 = 0.0144667.

Step 2. 1 + r × n = 1.0144667.

Step 3. C = 16,738.700 / 1.0144667 = 16,500 TND.

Check: interest = 16,500 × 0.084 × 62/360 = 238.700. 16,500 + 238.700 = 16,738.700 ✓.

Common mistake · Future value is not the interest

A = C + I. If the question asks "how much will you have" or "how much must you repay", add the capital. The interest alone is a trap option.

Common mistake · Present value: divide, do not subtract

Wrong way: take r × n of the future sum and subtract it. For 5,000 in 9 months at 6%: 5,000 − 5,000 × 0.045 = 4,775. This is not your teacher's present value (that method is "bank discount", a later chapter).

Right way (teacher's formula F5): C = A / (1 + r × n) = 5,000 / 1.045 = 4,784.689.

The average rate

Sometimes money is split into several placements. Each one has its own capital, rate and time.

The average rate is the one single rate that would give the same total interest on all the placements, each for its own time.

We use it to compare: is this mix better than one simple offer?

rA = Σ(C × r × n) / Σ(C × n)

Slides (F7): rA = Σ(Pi × ri × ni) / Σ(Pi × ni). Same formula, because C = P.

Σ means "add them all". Top: add every C × r × n. Bottom: add every C × n (the same, without r).

Memory trick · Top with r, bottom without r

Top = the interests (C × r × n). Bottom = the same products without r (C × n). Then divide.

You can use the days directly: the /360 is on the top and on the bottom, so it cancels.

Decision: 4.37% > 4% → choose the bank's offer.

P15Problem

Nour splits 10,000 TND: 3,000 TND at 6% for 90 days and 7,000 TND at 9% for 30 days. Find the average rate. Is it better than a single offer at 7.5%?

Show the full solution

Top: 3,000 × 0.06 × 90 = 16,200 · 7,000 × 0.09 × 30 = 18,900 → 35,100.

Bottom: 3,000 × 90 = 270,000 · 7,000 × 30 = 210,000 → 480,000.

rA = 35,100 / 480,000 = 0.073125 ≈ 7.31%.

7.31% < 7.5% → the single offer at 7.5% is better. (The simple average, 7.5%, would wrongly say "equal".)

Equations with two capitals

Some problems have two capitals and you must find them.

Two unknowns → you need two facts. Usually: (1) the sum of the capitals, and (2) the two interests are equal. (The slides call the capitals P1 and P2; here C1 and C2. Same thing.)

The recipe in 4 steps

  1. Write each interest with I = C × r × n. Keep fractions like 3/12 exact.
  2. Put I1 = I2. Cancel what is on both sides (the same rate r, the /12).
  3. Write C2 with C1, then put it into the sum.
  4. Check your answer in the first equation.
Memory trick · Cancel first, round never

If the same thing is on both sides (r, or /12), cross it out first. The numbers become small and you avoid rounding mistakes.

Example 1 · Exercise 3: two capitals, same interest

Two capitals add up to 7,000 TND, at the same rate. The first is placed for 3 months, the second for 4 months. They earn the same interest.

Step 1. I1 = C1 × r × 3/12 and I2 = C2 × r × 4/12.

Step 2. Equal: C1 × r × 3/12 = C2 × r × 4/12. Cancel r and /12 → 3 C1 = 4 C2 → C2 = 0.75 C1.

Step 3. C1 + 0.75 C1 = 7,000 → 1.75 C1 = 7,000 → C1 = 4,000 TND, and C2 = 3,000 TND.

Step 4. Check: 3 × 4,000 = 12,000 and 4 × 3,000 = 12,000 ✓.

Part 2. Which rate gives 60 TND each? 4,000 × r × 3/12 = 60 → 1,000 r = 60 → r = 6%. Check: 3,000 × 0.06 × 4/12 = 60 ✓.

Common mistake · The slide shows 3,977 and 3,023

The slide rounded 4/12 to 0.33 and then 0.25/0.33 to 0.76. So it found C1 = 3,977 and C2 = 3,023. With exact fractions the answer is 4,000 and 3,000 (and the check works perfectly). The 6% is right in both.

On the test: choose the exact answer (4,000 / 3,000). If only the slide's numbers are in the options, they come from this rounding.

Example 2 · Exercise 6: when are the two interests equal?

On 01/01/N, a first investment: 12,800 TND at 9%. Six months later (01/07/N), a second: 12,000 TND at 12%. Let n = the number of months of the first.

Step 1. I1 = 12,800 × 0.09 × n/12 = 96 n.

Step 2. The second runs 6 months less: I2 = 12,000 × 0.12 × (n − 6)/12 = 120 (n − 6).

Step 3. 96 n = 120 n − 720 → 24 n = 720 → n = 30 months.

Step 4. Date: 01/01/N + 12 months = 01/01/N+1; + 24 months = 01/01/N+2; + 30 months = 01/07/N+2 (at the end of 30 June N+2).

Check: I1 = 96 × 30 = 2,880 and I2 = 120 × 24 = 2,880 ✓.

Common mistake · The slide says June 30, N+3

That is a slip: 30 months after 01/01/N is 01/07/N+2, not N+3. Count it: 2 years and 6 months.

In French: equation = équation · two unknowns = deux inconnues · to check = vérifier.

Final test (25 questions)

Before you start: the 9 rules

  • TVM: 1 dinar today > 1 dinar later. Interest pays the lender for risk, liquidity, inflation (not the borrower's convenience).
  • Simple interest = only on the original capital: I = C × r × n (slides: P = C).
  • r as a decimal: 7% = 0.07. n in years: months / 12, days / 360.
  • Days: do not count the first day, count the last day. Feb 2026/2027 = 28, Feb 2028 = 29.
  • Ordinary = 360 (default, bigger). Exact = 365 (only if asked).
  • Future value: A = C + I = C × (1 + r × n). Present value: C = A / (1 + r × n) — divide, do not subtract.
  • Find r, n or C: I divided by the two letters you know. A date question needs a date.
  • Average rate: Σ(C × r × n) / Σ(C × n), never the simple average. End with a decision.
  • Two capitals: I1 = I2, cancel, keep fractions exact, check.

This is like your Moodle test: multiple choice, no notes, a calculator. Give yourself 35 minutes. Aim for 20/25 or more. If you miss one, read the explanation and go back to that section.
