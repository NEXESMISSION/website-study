01

Accounting in 2 minutes

Accounting is the language of business. It tells the story of a business with numbers.

Accounting does three things. It identifies money events. It records them. It communicates them in reports.

A money event that changes what the business owns or owes is called a transaction. We record only transactions.

Memory trick · Find it, write it, tell it

Identify = find the event. Record = write it in the books. Communicate = tell the users with reports.

DefinitionTransaction (opération)

An event that changes the assets, liabilities or equity of the business. Buying, selling, paying and borrowing are transactions.

In French: accounting = comptabilité · transaction = opération · to record = enregistrer.

Example · Café Zitouna in Sousse

Which events does the café record?

EventRecord it?Why
Buys a coffee machine for 3,000 TND cashYesCash goes down. Equipment goes up.
Sells coffees for 200 TND cashYesCash comes in. The café earned money.
Hires a waiter who starts next weekNoNo work done yet. Nothing paid, nothing owed.
The owner buys a car for his family with his own moneyNoThe owner is not the business.
Common mistake · The owner is not the business

The business is a separate "person". Its money is not the owner's money. Record only the business's events. This is the economic entity assumption (entité économique).

No change in what the business owns or owes = no transaction = nothing to record.

Quick check

Q1Multiple choice

What are the three activities of accounting?

Accounting identifies money events, records them, and communicates them in reports. Buying and selling are things the business does, not what accounting does.
Q2True or false

Café Zitouna buys a coffee machine for 3,000 TND cash. This is a transaction.

Something changed: the café has less cash and more equipment. A change in what the business owns = a transaction.
Q3True or false

The café hires a waiter who starts next month. The café must record this today.

Hiring is only an agreement. No work is done yet, nothing is paid, nothing is owed. Nothing changed, so there is no entry.
Q4Multiple choice

Youssef owns a phone shop. With his own money, he buys a TV for his home. What does the shop record?

The TV is Youssef's private property, paid with his private money. The shop is a separate entity, so the shop records nothing.
Q5Multiple choice

Which event is a transaction for a bakery?

Paying the bill changes the bakery's cash: that is a transaction. Ordering, talking and planning change nothing yet: no money moved and nothing is owed.
Q6True or false

"Record" means writing the transaction in the company's books.

Yes. Recording = writing it down in the books, so it is not forgotten. Later it is summarized and reported (communicate).
02

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Every business has things: cash, machines, supplies. These are its assets.

Who paid for these things? Two groups. The creditors (people the business owes money to) and the owners (the shareholders).

What the business owes = liabilities. The owners' part = equity.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

In French: Assets = Actif · Liabilities = Passif (dettes) · Equity = Capitaux propres.

Memory trick · Owns = Owes + Owners

What the business owns = what it owes + what belongs to the owners.

The accounts you need

Learn these names exactly. They come back in every question.

Memory trick · Receivable vs Payable

Receivable: we will receive money. Payable: we will pay money. "On account" means "pay later" (à crédit).

Example 1 · Find the missing number

Café Zitouna has cash of 5,000 TND and a coffee machine of 3,000 TND. It owes the bank 2,000 TND.

Step 1. Assets = 5,000 + 3,000 = 8,000.

Step 2. Liabilities = 2,000.

Step 3. Equity = Assets − Liabilities = 8,000 − 2,000 = 6,000 TND.

Example 2 · The equation stays balanced

Sousse Mobile, a new phone shop (amounts in TND).

  1. Owners invest 10,000 cash for shares. Cash +10,000, Share Capital +10,000.
  2. Buys equipment for 4,000 on account. Equipment +4,000, Accounts Payable +4,000.
  3. Pays 1,000 of what it owes. Cash −1,000, Accounts Payable −1,000.

Watch the totals after each event:

AfterAssets= Liab.+ Equity

Event 214,0004,00010,000

Check each line: 10,000 = 0 + 10,000 · 14,000 = 4,000 + 10,000 · 13,000 = 3,000 + 10,000.

After every transaction, the two sides are still equal.

Quick check · Which type?

Q7Which type?

Cash is:

Cash is something the business owns. Everything the business owns or controls is an asset.
Q8Which type?

Accounts Payable is:

"Payable" = we will pay. It is money we owe to a supplier, so it is a liability.
Q9Which type?

Share Capital—Ordinary is:

It is the money the owners put in to get shares. It is the owners' part, so it is equity.
Q10Which type?

Accounts Receivable is:

"Receivable" = we will receive money. A customer owes us. A right to receive money is an asset.
Q11Which type?

Unearned Service Revenue is:

The customer paid before the work. Now we owe the customer a service. Owing something = a liability.
Q12Calculate

Café Zitouna has assets of 20,000 TND and liabilities of 8,000 TND. What is its equity?

Equity = Assets − Liabilities = 20,000 − 8,000 = 12,000. Do not add them: 28,000 is wrong.
Q13Calculate

A phone shop has liabilities of 3,000 TND and equity of 9,000 TND. What are its assets?

Assets = Liabilities + Equity = 3,000 + 9,000 = 12,000.
Q14Multiple choice

A bakery buys supplies for 2,000 TND on account. What happens to the equation?

Supplies (an asset) go up by 2,000. "On account" = we will pay later, so Accounts Payable (a liability) also goes up by 2,000. Both sides +2,000: still balanced.
Q15Multiple choice

The bakery now pays the 2,000 TND it owes for the supplies. What happens?

Cash (asset) goes down 2,000. The debt (Accounts Payable, a liability) disappears: −2,000. It is not an expense: equity does not change.
Q16True or false

The accounting equation must balance after every transaction.

Always. Every transaction changes at least two things, so the two sides of the equation stay equal.
03

What an account is (T-account)

An account is a record for one item. It shows when this item goes up and when it goes down.

The business has one account for Cash, one for Equipment, one for Accounts Payable, and so on.

We draw an account like the letter T. The name is on top. The left side is the debit side. The right side is the credit side.

In French: account = compte · T-account = compte en T · Debit = Débit · Credit = Crédit.

Memory trick · cRedit = Right

Credit has an R, like Right. So credit = right side, and debit = left side.

Common mistake · Debit is not 'bad'

Debit only means left. Credit only means right. Alone, they do not mean good or bad, plus or minus. Up or down depends on the type of account (next section).

How to find the balance

1. Add the debit side. 2. Add the credit side. 3. Big total − small total. 4. The balance goes on the side with the bigger total.

Debits: 10,000 and 8,000. Credit: 3,000.

Quick check

Q17Multiple choice

In a T-account, the left side is called:

Left = debit (Dr). Right = credit (Cr). Remember: cRedit = Right.
Q18True or false

"Credit" always means something good for the business.

Credit only means right side. For Cash, a credit means cash goes down. That is not "good".
Q19Multiple choice

How many parts does a T-account have?

Three parts: (1) the title, (2) the left side = debit, (3) the right side = credit.
Q20True or false

An account is a record of the increases and decreases of one specific item.

Yes. Each item (Cash, Supplies, Rent Expense...) has its own account.
Q21Calculate

Cash: debits 6,000 and 2,000; credit 3,000. What is the balance?

Debits = 6,000 + 2,000 = 8,000. Credits = 3,000. 8,000 − 3,000 = 5,000. The debit side is bigger, so it is a debit balance.
Q22Calculate

Accounts Payable: credit 4,000; debit 1,500. What is the balance?

Credits 4,000 − debits 1,500 = 2,500. The credit side is bigger: 2,500 credit.
Q23Calculate

Supplies: debit 900; credits 300 and 200. What is the balance?

Debits 900. Credits 300 + 200 = 500. 900 − 500 = 400 debit.
Q24Multiple choice

The credit total of an account is bigger than its debit total. The account has:

The balance is always on the side with the bigger total. Credits bigger = credit balance. It is normal, not an error.
04

Debit and credit rules

Now the big rule. Look at the equation. Assets are on the left of the = sign. Liabilities and Equity are on the right.

Assets go up on the left side: debit. Liabilities and equity go up on the right side: credit.

To make an account go down, use the other side.

Asset ↑ = Debit · Liability ↑ = Credit · Equity ↑ = Credit
TypeGoes up withGoes down withNormal balance
Asset (Cash)DebitCreditDebit
Liability (Accounts Payable)CreditDebitCredit
Equity (Share Capital...)CreditDebitCredit
Memory trick · The = sign is a mirror

Left of = (assets) grows on the left = debit. Right of = (liabilities, equity) grows on the right = credit.

DefinitionNormal balance (solde normal)

The side where the account goes up. Cash normally has a debit balance. Accounts Payable normally has a credit balance.

DefinitionDouble entry (partie double)

Every transaction touches at least two accounts: at least one debit and at least one credit. Total debits = total credits.

In French: debit = Débit · credit = Crédit · double entry = partie double.

Memory trick · The 4 questions

For every transaction ask: 1. What changes? 2. Which accounts? 3. Up or down? 4. Debit or credit? Then check: debits = credits.

Example 1 · Owners invest 10,000 TND

Sousse Mobile's owners put 10,000 TND cash into the shop and get shares.

1. What changes? Cash comes in; the owners get shares.

2. Accounts? Cash (asset) and Share Capital—Ordinary (equity).

3. Up or down? Both go up.

4. Asset up → debit Cash 10,000. Equity up → credit Share Capital—Ordinary 10,000.

Check: 10,000 = 10,000.

Example 2 · Pay a supplier 1,000 TND

Sousse Mobile pays 1,000 TND that it owed to a supplier.

1–2. Accounts Payable (liability) and Cash (asset).

3. The debt goes down. Cash goes down.

4. Liability down → debit Accounts Payable. Asset down → credit Cash.

Equipment is an asset. The normal balance is the side where it goes up: debit.

Notes Payable is a liability (a loan we owe). Liabilities go up with a credit, so the normal balance is credit.

Q36Which entry?

Cash (asset) goes up → debit. The bakery now owes the bank with a written note → Notes Payable (liability) goes up → credit. The bank is not an owner, so not Share Capital.
05

Revenue, expenses, dividends

Equity has two parts: Share Capital—Ordinary (what owners put in) and Retained Earnings (profits kept).

Three things change retained earnings. Revenue makes it go up. Expenses and dividends make it go down.

  • Revenue: money earned by doing the work. Account: Service Revenue.
  • Expenses: costs of running the business: Rent Expense, Salaries and Wages Expense, Utilities Expense (STEG, SONEDE).
  • Dividends: profit given to the shareholders. Account: Dividends.

Revenue increases equity → it goes up with a credit. Expenses and dividends decrease equity → they go up with a debit.

Memory trick · DEALER

Dividends, Expenses, Assets → go up with a DEBIT (left).

Liabilities, Equity, Revenue → go up with a CREDIT (right).

The normal balance is on the same side. DEA = debit · LER = credit.

Assets = Liabilities + Share Capital + Retained Earnings − Dividends + Revenues − Expenses
Account typeGoes up withNormal balance
DividendsDebitDebit
ExpensesDebitDebit
AssetsDebitDebit
LiabilitiesCreditCredit
Equity (Share Capital, Retained Earnings)CreditCredit
RevenueCreditCredit

In French: revenue = produits · expenses = charges · dividends = dividendes · retained earnings = résultats non distribués · share capital = capital social.

The 5 classic traps
  1. Dividends are NOT expenses. Debit Dividends, never an expense account.
  2. Cash received before the work = Unearned Service Revenue (a liability). It is not revenue yet.
  3. Collecting from a customer who already owed us = NOT revenue. Debit Cash, credit Accounts Receivable.
  4. Paying a bill already recorded (Accounts Payable) = NOT an expense. Debit Accounts Payable, credit Cash.
  5. Hiring or ordering = NO entry. Nothing has changed yet.

Cash (asset) up → debit. Service Revenue (revenue) up → credit.

Rent Expense is:

Rent is a cost of running the business. Costs used up to earn revenue are expenses.

Revenue increases equity, and equity goes up with a credit. DEALER: R is on the credit side.

Dividends are an expense.

Classic trap. Dividends are profit given to the owners. They are not a cost of earning revenue. They reduce retained earnings but they are not expenses.

Which accounts normally have a debit balance?

Electricity is an expense: Utilities Expense up → debit. Paid in cash: Cash down → credit.
Q46Multiple choice

The work is not done yet, so it is not revenue. RepairPro now owes a repair: Unearned Service Revenue (a liability) goes up → credit. Cash is debited.

The work is done, so revenue is earned now → credit Service Revenue. No cash yet: the school owes us → Accounts Receivable (asset) up → debit.

Trap! The revenue was already recorded last month. Now cash comes in (debit Cash) and the school no longer owes us (credit Accounts Receivable). Collecting is not new revenue.

Which is the correct expanded equation?

Things that increase equity have +: share capital, retained earnings, revenues. Things that decrease equity have −: dividends and expenses. (In-class sheet question.)
06

Writing journal entries

The recording process has 3 steps: 1. Analyze the transaction (the 4 questions). 2. Journalize: write it in the journal. 3. Post: copy it to the accounts (next section).

The journal is the first book where a transaction is written. It is the book of original entry. Entries are in date order, like a diary.

Each transaction written in it is a journal entry.

In French: journal = Journal · journal entry = écriture comptable · to journalize = passer une écriture.

How to write an entry

  1. Write the date.
  2. Write the debit account first, on the left, with its amount in the Debit column.
  3. Write the credit account on the next line, pushed to the right, with its amount in the Credit column.
  4. Add a short explanation below.

No "TND" sign in the journal: only numbers.

Memory trick · Debit first, credit steps right

The debit is written first. The credit moves a little to the right, because cRedit = Right.

Example 1 · A simple entry (2 accounts)

Boulangerie Amal buys a new oven for 6,000 TND cash on 2 May.

What changes? Equipment up (asset → debit). Cash down (asset → credit).

Sousse Mobile buys repair machines for 5,000 TND on 4 May. It pays 2,000 cash and will pay 3,000 later.

DefinitionSimple and compound entry (écriture simple / composée)

A simple entry has 2 accounts. A compound entry has 3 or more accounts. The debit total must still equal the credit total.

Example 3 · Supplies on account

Boulangerie Amal buys paper bags (supplies) for 700 TND on account.

The bags are not used yet, so they are an asset: Supplies, not Supplies Expense. "On account" → Accounts Payable up → credit.

No entry!

Hiring someone, ordering goods, signing a contract for later, an interview: nothing changes yet. Write no entry.

07

Posting to the ledger

The journal lists transactions by date. But how much cash do we have now? For that we need the accounts.

The ledger is the group of all the accounts of the company. Each account shows its own balance.

Posting = copying each journal line to its account in the ledger. A debit line goes to the debit side of that account. A credit line goes to the credit side.

In French: ledger = Grand livre · posting = report au grand livre · chart of accounts = plan comptable.

Memory trick · Diary and folders

Journal = a diary (by date). Ledger = folders, one per account. Posting = moving each line from the diary into the right folder.

DefinitionChart of accounts (plan comptable)

The list of the company's accounts with their numbers. Example: 101 Cash, 112 Accounts Receivable, 126 Supplies, 157 Equipment, 201 Accounts Payable, 311 Share Capital—Ordinary, 400 Service Revenue, 729 Rent Expense.

After posting a line, write the account number in the Ref. column of the journal. It shows: "this line is done".

09

The trial balance

At the end of the month, we make a list of all the accounts and their balances. This list is the trial balance.

Debit balances go in the Debit column. Credit balances go in the Credit column. The two totals must be equal.

In French: trial balance = Balance (balance de vérification).

3 steps

  1. List each account and its balance (in ledger order: assets, liabilities, equity, revenues, expenses).
  2. Add the Debit column. Add the Credit column.
  3. Check that the two totals are equal.

Why make it? It proves that debits = credits. It helps find errors. It helps prepare the financial statements.

Memory trick · DEALER again

Debit column: Dividends, Expenses, Assets. Credit column: Liabilities, Equity, Revenue.

The TND sign is used only in the trial balance and the financial statements. Never in the journal or the ledger.

If the totals are not equal: where is the mistake?

  • First, add the columns again.
  • Is the difference divisible by 2? Maybe an amount was put on the wrong side. Example: a 180 debit written as a credit → difference 360.
  • Is the difference divisible by 9? Maybe two digits were swapped. Example: 1,250 written as 1,520 → difference 270 = 9 × 30.

A trial balance is:

Q83Multiple choice

Q84Multiple choice

Dividends have a debit normal balance (the D of DEALER). They go in the Debit column, even though they are part of equity.
Q85Multiple choice

In which column does Accounts Receivable go?

Accounts Receivable is an asset (customers owe us). Assets → Debit column.
Q86Calculate

If the trial balance balances, all transactions were recorded correctly.

Moving 180 from the debit side to the credit side makes debits 180 smaller and credits 180 bigger: difference = 2 × 180 = 360. The other errors do not change the totals.

No currency signs in the journal or the ledger. They appear in the trial balance and the financial statements (on the first number and on the total).

10

Errors the trial balance can't catch

A balanced trial balance does not mean the books are perfect.

It only checks that debits = credits. If a mistake changes both sides by the same amount, the totals stay equal. The trial balance cannot see it.

Equal mistakes hide. Unequal mistakes show.

5 errors the trial balance can't catch

  1. A transaction is never journalized (forgotten).
  2. A correct journal entry is never posted.
  3. A journal entry is posted twice.
  4. The wrong account is used (but on the correct side).
  5. The same wrong amount is used for the debit and the credit.
Memory trick · Same on both sides = hidden

Ask: "Did this mistake change the debit total and the credit total by the same amount?" Yes → the trial balance still balances. No → it does not balance.

In French: error = erreur · posted twice = reporté deux fois · forgotten = oublié.

Example · 3 mistakes at Café Zitouna
  1. The café forgot to record a 700 TND sale. → Nothing on either side. Still balances (but wrong).
  2. It paid 450 TND to a supplier and wrote 45 for the debit and 45 for the credit. → Both sides too small by 405. Still balances.
  3. It recorded a 100 TND dividend as debit 1,000 and credit 100. → Debits are 900 bigger. Does not balance.

If a correct journal entry is posted twice, the trial balance will not balance.

Posting twice adds the same amount to the debits and to the credits. Totals stay equal → it still balances, but the balances are wrong.

The same wrong amount (45) is on both sides. Debits and credits are each 405 too small, so the totals are still equal.

Wrong account, but the right side (credit) and the right amount. Debits = credits, so it balances. Cash and Accounts Payable are both wrong.

The debit total loses 300 and the credit total gains 300. Difference = 2 × 300 = 600, credits bigger. (Tip: divisible by 2 → wrong side.)

Final test (20 questions)

Before you start: the 8 rules

  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity, always.
  • Debit = left, credit = right.
  • DEALER: Dividends, Expenses, Assets go up with a debit. Liabilities, Equity, Revenue go up with a credit.
  • Every entry: total debits = total credits.
  • Cash before the work = Unearned Service Revenue (liability).
  • Collecting from a customer or paying a supplier = no revenue, no expense.
  • Dividends are not expenses. Hiring and ordering = no entry.
  • Journal (by date) → posting → ledger (by account) → trial balance (debits = credits, but not proof of no errors).
